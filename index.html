Awaken to your inner wilderness

A guided wilderness walk

A self-led nature walk. On your own, or with friends. Come as you are.

Welcome

However you're arriving, that's fine

This is a 20-minute guided walk with a recording to listen to, followed by time to reflect and write. You can do it on your own, or with a small group you trust.

The recording is a gentle introduction to wilderness therapy exercises, and to thinking in metaphor: letting something in nature act as a mirror for what's going on in you. Once you're back, there are a few more exercises to try further down the page.

Different parts of you might turn up today. A curious part. An anxious part. A tired part, or one that's a bit sceptical about all this. They're all welcome. Nobody needs to be left at home.

Everything here is an invitation. Follow it, change it, or let it go by. You can pause or stop at any point.

This is a wellbeing practice. It isn't therapy, and it isn't crisis support.

Walking with friends

Holding the space

If you're doing this together, read these out before you set off. They keep it safe for everyone.

Just listen

No advising, no fixing, no rescuing. You don't need to make anyone feel better. Being there is enough.

Share what you want

Nobody has to say anything. Passing is always fine, and nobody asks why.

No comments

When someone shares, let it land. No "at least", no stories of your own, no questions to dig deeper.

It stays here

What's shared on the walk isn't passed on to anyone else.

A stop signal

Agree one before you start, like a raised hand. Anyone can use it, any time, no explanation needed.

Nobody walks off alone

If someone needs to stop or head back, someone goes with them, or the group goes together.

Keep an eye out

Pick one person to keep an eye on the group and the route. They can still take part.

If it gets too much

Five steps, any time

  1. Stop. Somewhere out of the way.
  2. Eyes open. Look around slowly.
  3. Name three things you can see. Out loud if it helps.
  4. Get solid. Feet on the ground, or a hand on a tree, wall or bench.
  5. Choose. Carry on, walk back, or stop the recording.

Reflections

Space to write

Write as much or as little as you like. Notes, single words, half sentences. None of it is marked.

What did you notice as you set off?

Anything that caught your eye, or your ear?

What were you noticing when you stopped? Has anything shifted, or does it feel much the same? Either is fine.

Which parts of you showed up today? The curious part, the anxious part, the part that wasn't sure. No need to change any of them.

Nature as a mirror

Ways in

Pick one, some, or none. Start with what's actually there. Only then, if you'd like, ask whether it connects with anything in your life. Whatever it means is yours to find. If nothing comes, that's fine.

You can pick things up if you like. Leave anything still growing where it is, and put things back when you're done if you can.

One

From the walk

The thing you settled on during the recording. A tree, a stone, a puddle, a weed in a wall. Whatever kept drawing you back.

Shape, colour, how it sits, any marks or wear.

Only if you'd like to. You might ask: what draws me to this? Does it feel familiar? Does it bring a question or a possibility to mind?

Two

How I see myself, how I'd like to be seen

Take a little longer outside, or use things you picked up or remember. Find two natural things. Let the first stand for how you see yourself right now. Let the second stand for how you'd like to be seen.

Start with what's actually there: shape, colour, weight, marks. Then, if you like, what feels familiar about it. This is one view of yourself. It isn't the whole story.

A quality, a colour, the way it holds itself. Where does that already show up in you, even a little?

What's the same? What's different? There's nothing to fix. Just notice.

Three

Then, now, next

Find three natural things on your walk. Let each one stand for a part of your story, in whatever way makes sense to you.

Four

Just describe it

If words about meaning don't come, describe one thing in plain detail, as if to someone who's never seen it. Or sketch it on paper.

If any of this brings up more than you'd like, leave it. Look around, and go to the if you need to.

Strengths and values

What already helps

A few questions to wonder about. You don't need full answers. A word or two is plenty.

It can be small. Making a decent brew. Listening. Turning up. What would someone who knows you well say?

What was different? What were you doing?

Even a little. A person, a place, a habit.

When do you feel most like yourself?

Walking with friends

A sharing circle

An optional way to close if you're walking together. Read the Holding the space agreements out loud first.

  1. Gather. Stand or sit in a loose circle, somewhere out of the way.
  2. Pick a talking piece. A stick or a stone. Whoever holds it speaks. Put it back where you found it afterwards.
  3. One at a time. Each person gets a couple of minutes. Share something you noticed, your metaphor, or nothing.
  4. Just listen. No comments, no advice, no questions. When someone finishes, the group says "thank you" and that's all.
  5. Passing is fine. Hand the piece on without speaking if you'd rather.
  6. Close with one word. Go round once more. One word each for how you're leaving.

Goals to carry

One small thing

Is there one small thing you'd like to take into the rest of your day, or your week? A detail you noticed, a question, a cup of tea, a phone call. If nothing stands out, that's fine too.

If it doesn't happen, that's okay. You can come back to it.

Things to try

Steadying tools

All optional. If one doesn't feel right, skip it and try another, or none.

Look around

Turn your head slowly and let your eyes move. Name the colours you can see. Then shapes. Then anything that's moving.

Skip it if looking around feels like too much. Pick one spot instead.

5, 4, 3, 2, 1

Eyes open. Find 5 things you can see, 4 you can hear, 3 you can touch, 2 you can smell, and 1 thing you like about where you are.

If you lose count, that's fine. Start again, or just do the first one.

Get solid

Press your feet into the ground. Or put your hands on something steady: a tree, a wall, a bench, your own knees. Notice it holding your weight.

If noticing your body doesn't feel good today, stay with what you can see instead.

Slow steps

Walk a little slower and count your steps up to ten. Then start again at one. Keep your eyes on the path ahead.

Only where the ground is safe and you can see where you're going.

A longer breath out

Let your breath out be a little longer than your breath in. No need to breathe deeply. Just a slightly slower out-breath, a few times.

If paying attention to your breathing makes things worse, leave this one.

Use your voice

Hum quietly. Or say out loud where you are, what day it is, and what you can see. Plain and simple. "I'm in the park. It's Saturday. There's a bench and a bin."

Fine to do it under your breath if people are around.

Reach out

Send a short message to someone you trust. You don't have to explain.

Hey, having a rough moment. Can you give me a call when you get this?

Walking with friends

If a friend is struggling

  • Stay with them. Calm voice, no rush.
  • Offer simple choices: stop, sit down, walk back, call someone.
  • Help them look around and name what's here.
  • Don't fix, advise, or ask about what came up. Being there is enough.
  • If they're at risk, call 999 and stay with them.
  • Check in with them the next day.

After the walk

If something stays with you

Sometimes a walk brings things up for a few days. That's normal. Talking it through with your GP, a therapist, or someone you trust is a good next step. It isn't a sign anything went wrong.
